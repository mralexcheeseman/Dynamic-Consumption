# Making a page

**What this is.** How a Dynamic Consumption page gets made, start to finish. Dynamic Consumption is Alex Cheeseman's daily blog about consuming things with your eyes open: buying, reading, watching, eating, using. One page a day, about ten minutes, all on [`/write`](https://dynamic-consumption-dee615cf.base44.app/write).

## The idea in one line

**You supply the thing and what you noticed. The desk only shapes it.** Nothing starts from a blank page, and nothing is invented: if the desk needs a detail it doesn't have, it leaves a `[square bracket]` for you.

## During the day: catch (30 seconds each)

Something catches your eye: a thing you bought, read, watched, ate, used, overheard or saw. On `/write`, step 1:

1. Tap what kind it was.
2. Write what it was, **exactly**: where, how much, what it was like. *"A £3.80 flat white from the place by the station. Lid didn't fit. Drank half, binned the rest."*
3. **Save for later.** It goes in **the jar**.

The more specific, the better the page. Brand, price, place, temperature, texture.

## Once a day: the page (about 10 minutes)

| Step | What you do | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Catch** | Pick something from the jar (**Use**), or catch it now (**Write about this now**). No spark? **Start from a thought instead.** | 30 s |
| **2. React** | One or two lines: **what did you notice?** This is the page. Stuck? Answer one of the four prompts on screen. | 2 min |
| **3. Shape** | Pick **Note** (40–100 words, one turn), **Page** (150–250, scene → what you noticed → what it means) or **List** (4–6 items and a verdict). Then **Draft it for me**, or **I'll write it myself**. | 1 min |
| **4. Finish** | Edit on the sheet. Fill any `[brackets]`. Tap the one line to highlight. Try the **other last lines**. Not right? Type what to change and **Redraft**. Optional: photograph a two-to-five word margin note in red pen. **Publish today.** | 5 min |

Publishing puts the page on the site and emails it to subscribers (once a sending domain is set up). A **Save draft** keeps it under Drafts for tomorrow.

## The reminder

At 07:00 and 20:00 London time, if today's page isn't up, an email arrives with your streak and the oldest thing in the jar. Publish and it goes quiet.

## Rules the desk follows

- Your reaction is the argument. It is shaped, never swapped for a bigger idea.
- Only the facts in your spark and reaction. No invented memories, people, prices or quotes.
- Your voice: short punch after a long sentence, the two-beat reframe, dry wit through specifics, UK spelling.
- Never: em dashes, "not X but Y", consultant filler, hype, exclamation marks, stacked questions.

Two passes run on every draft: a writer, then an editor that checks the opening, the turn, the line worth repeating, truth, voice and ending, and rewrites what fails.
